Risk, return & diversification

The trade-off every investor faces — and how spreading bets (without guaranteeing safety) is the professional’s first defense.

Layman idea

Return is what you hope to earn. Risk is the chance you don’t get it — including losing some or all of the original investment. Per SEC Investor.gov: as risks rise, investors generally seek higher returns as compensation. Your risk tolerance is how much loss you can stomach; your time horizon is how long the money can stay invested.

Small story

Sam put his entire bonus into one hot tech stock. It doubled — then halved. Priya split hers across a broad stock fund, a bond fund, and cash. When tech crashed, Priya’s bonds and cash softened the blow. Sam learned the SEC’s line the hard way: don’t put all your eggs in one basket — diversification can’t promise no losses, but it can reduce how bad a single failure hurts.

Asset allocation vs diversification

  • Asset allocation — how you split money among stocks, bonds, cash (and maybe other classes). Personal; depends on horizon and risk tolerance.
  • Diversification — spreading money across and within asset classes so one bad holding doesn’t sink you.
  • Rebalancing — when winners grow too big (e.g. stocks drift from 60% → 80%), you trim/add to restore the target mix. That often means selling some winners and buying laggards — buy low, sell high by policy.

Mutual funds/ETFs make broad ownership easy, but a single-sector fund is not automatically diversified — check holdings (Investor.gov).

Simple return = (Ending − Beginning) ÷ Beginning

Interview add-on: higher expected return usually means accepting more uncertainty — there is no free lunch.
