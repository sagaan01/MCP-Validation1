Lesson 0004 · Finance & Wealth

The three financial statements

A company’s story in three cameras — performance over time, what it owns/owes now, and how cash actually moved.

Layman idea

Public companies file an annual Form 10-K with the SEC. Inside, Item “Financial Statements and Supplementary Data” holds the audited statements: income statement, balance sheet, and statement of cash flows (How to read a 10-K). U.S. statements follow GAAP; an independent accountant audits them.

Small story

Think of a neighborhood bakery. The income statement is the year’s scoreboard: sales of bread minus flour, rent, wages → profit or loss. The balance sheet is a snapshot tonight: ovens and cash (assets) vs loans and what owners have left (liabilities + equity). The cash flow statement is the CCTV on the register: even if the scoreboard shows profit, did cash actually pile up or get trapped in unpaid invoices and new ovens?

The map (memorize this table)

Statement Question it answers Time idea
Income statement Did we earn a profit? Period (year/quarter)
Balance sheet What do we own and owe? Point in time
Cash flow statement Where did cash come from / go? Period
Assets = Liabilities + Equity

Balance sheet identity — every asset is claimed by lenders or owners.

Net worth (personal) = Assets − Liabilities

Same logic for you: SEC’s starting wealth exercise is a personal balance sheet.

Interview ratios from these statements

Profit margin = Net income ÷ Revenue
Current ratio = Current assets ÷ Current liabilities
Debt-to-equity = Total debt ÷ Equity

Also read MD&A (management’s story) and Risk Factors in the 10-K — numbers without narrative miss half the interview answer.
